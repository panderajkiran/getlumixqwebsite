Lumi6 Operating Ethos

The Principles We Live By

The internal mental models, cultural norms, and relentless standards guiding everything we build at Lumi6.

Our Vision

We are building technology that changes the way people learn, practice, and develop human capability.

The current way people learn is often passive: watch, read, attend, remember, forget. We believe learning can be fundamentally different.

We want to build experiences that are dramatically better — 100× better in usefulness, engagement, personalization, and real-world impact than traditional ways of learning.

That ambition requires more than talented people. It requires a particular way of thinking. These are the principles we expect everyone at Lumi6 to live by — whether in engineering, product, design, content, sales, or anything else.

Accountability & Mindset

Own the Outcome

The Core Question
“What needs to be true for this to succeed, and what can I do to make it happen?”
What it means

You are responsible for the outcome, not just the task assigned to you. If something needs to happen, don't wait for someone to tell you every step.

Ownership means taking responsibility when things go wrong, giving visibility when you are blocked, and seeing problems beyond your immediate role.

✕ You Don't Say “That's not my job.”
✓ You Say “I noticed a problem. How can I help solve it?”
Why it matters

Early-stage companies cannot operate through layers of instructions and approvals. Lumi6 needs people who think like builders and owners.

What it looks like
Low Ownership “I finished what you asked me to do.”
High Ownership “I finished it, noticed two problems, fixed one, and have a suggestion for the other.”

02
Initiative & Momentum

High Agency — Don't Wait to Be Told

The Agency Mindset
High agency means believing that you can influence the outcome and then taking immediate action.
What it means
When you don't know something → Learn it.
When you don't have a tool → Find one.
When you don't have an answer → Investigate.
When you see an opportunity → Pursue it.
When you are blocked → Communicate and find another path.
Why it matters

At Lumi6, you will often receive incomplete instructions. That is intentional.

We Transform This “I don't know how.”
Into This “I don't know yet. Let me figure it out.”
What it looks like

• Don't wait three days for an answer to a problem you could research in thirty minutes.

• Bring questions + possible solutions, not only questions.

03
Reasoning & Invention

Think From First Principles

Zero-Base Reasoning
Don't accept assumptions because “that's how everyone does it”. Break problems down to fundamental truths.
The 5 First Principles Questions
1
What are we actually trying to achieve?
2
What do we know to be true?
3
What are we assuming?
4
What constraints are real?
5
If we started from zero, how would we solve this?
Why it matters

Lumi6 is trying to build something that doesn't already have a perfect blueprint. We cannot simply copy existing learning platforms. We need to understand the fundamental problem and invent from there.

Practical Example
Don't Ask “How should an AI learning dashboard look?”
Ask This “What does a learner actually need to understand, decide, and do at this moment?”
→ The interface follows the answer.

04
Empathy & Purpose

Start With the User

Customer Obsession
Build for the person using the product — not for ourselves. The user's experience is the ultimate test.
The Grounding Question
Before building something, ask:
“Who is this for, and what problem are we solving for them?”

Understand their context, frustrations, motivations, behaviour, and desired outcome.

Why it matters — The 3 Failure Tests
Still a Failure A technically impressive product that nobody wants.
Still a Failure A beautiful design that doesn't help the user.
Still a Failure A clever AI response that doesn't improve learning.
What it looks like
Instead Of “I built this feature.”
Think “What does this enable the user to do better?”

Velocity & Iteration

Bias Toward Action

The Iteration Loop
Don't spend weeks discussing something that can be tested in a day. Thoughtful speed beats endless debate.
When Risk is Low & Decision is Reversible
1. Build
2. Test
3. Learn
4. Improve
Why it matters

Most ideas sound good in a meeting. Reality tells us whether they actually work. The fastest way to learn is often to put something in front of a real user.

Speed doesn't mean carelessness
Move Quickly On experiments, prototypes, and low-cost reversible decisions.
Move Carefully On decisions that are expensive, irreversible, or affect users significantly.

Design & Elegance

Simplify Everything

Clarity over Complexity
Complexity is easy. Simplicity is hard. We should constantly ask: “Can this be simpler?”
Where Simplicity Applies

Product • UI • Code • Communication • Processes • Meetings • Documentation • Learning experiences.

Why it matters

Our goal is to create experiences that feel almost obvious. The best products often hide enormous complexity behind simple experiences.

Our Design Test
The Removal Test If we can remove something without reducing value, remove it.
The Intuition Test If a user needs an explanation to understand our interface, ask whether the interface itself can be improved.

08
Intellectual Rigor

Challenge Ideas, Not People

Truth-Seeking Culture
The best idea should win — not the idea of the most senior person in the room. Disagree, debate, then commit fully.
What it means

We want strong opinions. We also want people to change their minds when the evidence changes.

If you disagree, say so. Explain why. Bring evidence. Listen. Debate the idea. Once a decision is made, commit to it fully.

Why it matters

A healthy company needs disagreement. It also needs alignment after decisions are made.

What it looks like
Don't Say “I don't think this will work.”
Say This “I disagree because of X. I think Y would be a better approach because…”

The Quality Standard
Good enough is not our default. We obsess over the details that users experience.

Execution & Tangibility

Build, Don't Just Discuss

The Builder Ethos
Ideas are cheap. Prototypes, experiments, working software, user conversations, and evidence are valuable.
What it means

When possible, make the idea tangible. Don't spend ten meetings discussing whether something might work. Build a version. Put it in front of someone. Learn.

Why it matters

Lumi6 is a company of builders. We don't want to merely talk about the future of learning. We want to build it.

Standards & Work Ethic

Professionalism & Time Punctuality

Respect For Time & Commitments
“Being on time and fully prepared is not a minor formality — it is the absolute bedrock of trust, velocity, and mutual respect.”
What it means

Professionalism is how we honor our word, respect our teammates and users, and execute with consistency. Being on time is an uncompromising standard of respect for everyone's focus and energy.

Punctuality means arriving early or on time for every meeting, session, and deadline, fully prepared with context, questions, and solutions.

If an unavoidable delay arises, communicate proactively hours ahead—never after the meeting has already started.

✕ You Don't Say “Sorry I'm late, I lost track of time” / “I forgot about the call.”
✓ You Say “I respect everyone's calendar. I'm here early, prepared with the agenda, and ready to deliver.”
Why it matters

High-velocity organizations are built on uncompromising trust. When people are punctual and professional, discussions are sharp, momentum compounds, and everyone can count on each other without second-guessing.

What it looks like
Low Professionalism Joining 5 minutes late without warning, multitasking in meetings, unprepared, and silently missing deadlines.
High Professionalism Arriving 2 minutes early, tech tested, agendas pre-read, delivering on commitments or proactively communicating roadblocks early.
